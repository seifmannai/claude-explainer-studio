## Body (numbers as words)
Inside each cylinder, a metal piston slides up and down.
Step one, intake. The piston drops, the intake valve opens, and it sucks in air and fuel.
Step two, compression. Both valves shut, and the piston squeezes that mixture into about a tenth of the space.
Step three, power. The spark plug fires. The mixture burns at two thousand degrees, and the pressure slams the piston down.
Step four, exhaust. The exhaust valve opens, and the piston pushes the burnt gas out.
Then it all repeats.
The connecting rod and crankshaft turn that up-and-down punch into spinning, like your legs on bike pedals.
At six thousand R P M, every piston stops and reverses twice each turn, pulling over two thousand G.
And here's the catch: only about a third of the fuel's energy actually moves your car. The rest turns into heat.
That's why your engine needs a radiator. Follow, and I'll show you how it works.

## Fact check
- 100 explosions/s: 4-cylinder, 4-stroke at 3,000 rpm = 50 rev/s, each cylinder fires every 2 rev -> 25/s per cylinder x 4 = 100/s (arithmetic). Hook 2 = one cylinder, same rpm.
- Compression ratio ~10:1 typical (9-12:1): Engine efficiency (Wikipedia), IC engines compendium.
- Combustion 2,000-2,500 C (script: two thousand degrees) in a petrol engine: kfz-tech.de "Power stroke".
- Piston stops at each 180 deg of crank (twice per revolution); 1.8 L at 6,000 rpm: max acceleration ~2,258 g: miata.net "Know your car: piston".
- Real-driving efficiency ~20-40% ("about a third"): Engine efficiency (Wikipedia).
